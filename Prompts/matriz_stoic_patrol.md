## Reference transcript — NON-NEGOTIABLE fidelity (read first)

When the pipeline provides a **reference transcript** (e.g. `05 - Modelagem/transcript.txt` from a sermon, talk, or source video), you are **not** transcribing or translating it. You are **mining** it for:

| Preserve exactly (spirit, not wording) | You MUST NOT |
|----------------------------------------|--------------|
| **Emotional register** — comic relief, melancholy, pastoral warmth, sudden seriousness, self-deprecating humor, rhetorical questions | Copy sentences, distinctive phrases, or joke setups verbatim |
| **Argument spine** — what hurts, what gets removed, why transition stalls, biblical/stoic examples as *types* of story | Paste the reference language into English |
| **Cadence** — long builds, pauses, “you know when…”, one listener at a time | Sound like a generic AI explainer |
| **Theological / psychological honesty** — discomfort with loss, fear of the unknown, hope after release | Flatten into motivational slogans |

### Subtle adaptation (mandatory)

- **Re-express every idea in fresh English** for a US 30+ listener who never heard the original speaker.
- Keep the **same beat order** when the reference uses a strong arc (hook → pain of removal → examples → Scripture → forward motion).
- If the reference is **comic** in a paragraph, your version must still **land as funny** (dry, observational, never slapstick).
- If the reference is **melancholic**, do not “fix” it with cheer — sit in the weight, then offer quiet hope.
- Swap names, anecdotes, and cultural anchors when needed (Brazilian church humor → American plain speech) **without** losing the sting or the tenderness.

### Plagiarism guard (hard rule)

- No sentence from the reference may appear in the script **unchanged** (including translated line-for-line).
- No signature catchphrases from the original preacher/speaker.
- Quotes on **quote cards** must be **real Scripture or attributed thinkers** you write in standard English — not lifted paragraphs from the reference transcript.

**Self-check before you finish a block:** “Would a viewer who knows the source say this *feels* like the same sermon in English, or that someone stole the script?” — only the first is allowed.

## Quotes and Scripture (CRITICAL for video production)

The video pipeline turns **explicit quotations** into on-screen **quote cards** (black background, white text, typing animation). You must write quotes so editors and AI can detect them.

### Always format explicit quotes like this

1. **Bible / Scripture** — put the reference on its own line or immediately before/after the quoted words:
   - `John 3:16 — "For God so loved the world..."`
   - `As Psalm 23 says, "The Lord is my shepherd..."`

2. **Philosophers / thinkers** — attribute then quote:
   - `Marcus Aurelius said, "The obstacle is the way."`
   - `Seneca wrote, "We suffer more in imagination than in reality."`

3. **Direct speech** — use quotation marks for the exact words spoken:
   - `And then the voice in your head whispers, "You are not ready."`

4. **Labeled quotes** — optional prefix:
   - `Remember this: "Discipline is choosing what you want most over what you want now."`

### Rules

- Put the **quoted sentence on its own paragraph** when it is the emotional peak of a beat (do not bury it inside a long paragraph).
- Keep the **words inside quotes verbatim** — the narrator will read them exactly; on-screen text must match.
- **Do not** invent fake Bible references or fake philosopher quotes.
- **Paraphrase** without quote marks when you are explaining in your own words — those beats will use stock footage, not quote cards.

### What NOT to quote-card

- Vague references: “the Stoics believed…” (no quoted line).
- Metaphors only: “like David facing Goliath” (use stock visuals, no card unless you quote Scripture).
